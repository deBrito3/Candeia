# The first night he came

You were six, with a fever that would not go down. The power went in the middle of the storm, as it does every year, and I went to the kitchen for candles. When I came back, your bedroom window was open, and there was a sweet on the sill.

That window does not open from outside. I shut it, lit the candles around your bed and did not sleep again.

He did not come in. He stayed in the yard until daybreak, standing at the edge of the candlelight. That is when I understood the only thing I know about him to this day: he does not cross the light.

But candles burn out, and people sleep. So I learned to program, at night, on the computers of the office where I worked. A house that lights itself does not forget, does not tire and does not sleep.
